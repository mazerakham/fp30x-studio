Cut, glue, and L²([0,1], V)

Where the homeomorphism assumption should be dropped, what it should be replaced with, and one place the vector-valued picture earns its keep outright. 2026-08-26.

The short version. Yes to all of it, with one correction. The literature you want is jump-aware alignment, and the right generalisation of $\varphi$ is not a looser homeomorphism but a monotone relation — which is exactly what a DTW path already is. Your instinct about pricing cuts has a clean formulation: charge a cut for the mass it destroys, not the duration it skips, and snipping silence becomes free with no special case. And the $L^2([0,1],V)$ picture buys one thing decisively — it makes gluing well-defined, retiring the pedal-chasing hack. But $L^2$ is the wrong norm to measure with, and the fix has a name.

1. The literature exists, and it is your question

JumpDTW — Fremerey, Müller & Clausen, Handling Repeats and Jumps in Score-Performance Synchronization, ISMIR 2010 (PDF, dblp). The score is cut into blocks at structurally licensed points; the DTW cost matrix is augmented with jump edges between block boundaries, and the jump sequence and the alignment are recovered in one optimisation. Over 99% bar-level accuracy on Beethoven sonatas. This is precisely "a minimal set of transformations $T_1,\dots,T_N$", with the transformation set restricted to block-boundary jumps so the search stays polynomial.

Follow-ons worth having: Improved Handling of Repeats and Jumps in Audio–Sheet Image Synchronization (ISMIR 2020) and Brazier & Widmer, Handling Structural Mismatches in Real-time Opera Tracking (EUSIPCO 2021), which is the online case.

The discrete ancestor is Gotoh (1982): affine gap penalties, cost $g(k) = \alpha + \beta k$ — expensive to open a gap, cheap to extend it. Your correspond.py is already Smith–Waterman, i.e. local alignment with linear gaps. Affine is a small change to _local_align and is strictly the better model: one long cut should not cost what many short ones cost.

2. Price the cut by mass, not by duration

This is the part of your question with the cleanest answer. You asked for a measure "not too punishing of snipping on the left and right endpoint of a section of silence". Do not special-case silence. Charge a cut for what it removes:

$$ c(\text{cut over } [s,t]) \;=\; \int_s^t \lVert f(u) \rVert_V \, du $$

On silence $f \equiv 0$, so the cut is free — automatically, and by the same formula that makes cutting through a held chord expensive. Cheap at the edges of a rest, prohibitive mid-phrase, and continuous in between. No threshold to tune.

This is not an ad-hoc trick. It is the marginal-relaxation penalty of unbalanced optimal transport (Chizat–Peyré–Schmitzer–Vialard): allow mass to be created and destroyed, and pay a divergence for the privilege. "This part of the recording corresponds to nothing in the score" is exactly unmatched mass.

3. What $L^2([0,1],V)$ actually buys

It makes gluing well-defined — this is the real prize

In the event representation a cut can orphan a note-off, and pedal state at the splice has to be reconstructed. That is why segment.py carries a Chase Events routine and why the skill warns that a clip cut mid-phrase renders dry.

In the function representation there is no such thing as an orphan. $f(t)$ is the sounding state — the vector of momenta currently sustained. Gluing $f|_{[a,b]}$ to $f|_{[c,d]}$ is concatenation of restrictions, and the state carried across the seam is just $f(c^-)$, obtained by evaluating the function. Held notes and pedal survive by construction.

Chasing is not an algorithm; it is evaluation. That single observation retires a hack you are currently maintaining, and it is the strongest argument for adopting the formulation.

Correlation is an inner product, and chroma is a projection

$L^2([0,1],V) \cong L^2([0,1]) \otimes V$ is a separable Hilbert space with $\langle f,g\rangle = \int_0^1 \langle f(t), g(t)\rangle_V\,dt$. The GEMM you liked is $\tau \mapsto \langle f, g(\cdot - \tau)\rangle$ — cross-correlation is literally the inner product of translates.

And it locates today's failure exactly. Chroma is the pushforward along the octave-quotient $\pi : \mathbb{R}^{88} \to \mathbb{R}^{12}$. Correlating chroma is correlating $\pi f$ against $\pi g$, and

$$ \dim \ker \pi = 76 .$$
The validation found that on a typical window the best wrong score beats the right one. That is not a warping failure. It is a 76-dimensional kernel — register was thrown away before the comparison started. See §7.

4. Where $L^2$ fails, and the two fixes

$L^2$ is a bad norm for alignment, and badly so. For step functions with sharp onsets, $\lVert f - f(\cdot-\varepsilon)\rVert_{L^2}$ does not decay as $\varepsilon \to 0$ in any useful way — two performances of the same music at slightly different tempi are nearly orthogonal. This is why nobody aligns in $L^2$ and everyone reaches for DTW.

Fix A — measure in a negative-order Sobolev norm

In $H^{-1}$, two Diracs a distance $\varepsilon$ apart are $O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})$ apart rather than a fixed distance. Onset jitter becomes cheap smoothly and automatically, instead of by a hand-set tolerance.

You currently have that tolerance as a constant: CLUSTER_TOL_S = 0.055. Replacing a magic number with a norm is a real improvement — the 30 ms chord window and the 55 ms cluster window are two guesses at the same underlying fact.

Fix B — quotient by the warping group (this is the rigorous version of what you built)

Let $\Gamma$ be the group of increasing diffeomorphisms of $[0,1]$. The square-root velocity function $q_f = \dot f / \sqrt{|\dot f|}$ turns the Fisher–Rao metric into the plain $L^2$ metric, and the key identity is that $\Gamma$ acts by isometries: $q_{f\circ\gamma} = (q_f\circ\gamma)\sqrt{\dot\gamma}$ preserves the norm. So

$$ d\big([f],[g]\big) \;=\; \inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\; \big\lVert\, q_f - (q_g\circ\gamma)\sqrt{\dot\gamma} \,\big\rVert_{L^2} $$

is a genuine metric on $L^2/\Gamma$ — performance modulo rubato — computable by dynamic programming. Srivastava, Klassen, Joshi & Jermyn, Registration of Functional Data Using Fisher-Rao Metric (arXiv:1103.3817); implementations in fdasrsf (Python) and fdasrvf (R).

Your warp.py is a cousin of this: you minimise $\sum_i(\varphi(t_i)-s_i)^2 + \lambda\!\int|\varphi'|^2$, Fisher–Rao effectively works with $\sqrt{\varphi'}$. The SRVF version buys something yours does not have — it is a metric. Your $E[\varphi]$ is asymmetric in $f$ and $g$, so it obeys no triangle inequality, which is why "align A to B" and "align B to A" can disagree and why you cannot cluster takes with it. That matters the moment you want to ask which of six attempts at the ending is the outlier.

5. The object you actually want is a monotone relation

Drop injectivity and continuity but keep order, and the right object is not a map at all. It is a closed monotone relation $R \subseteq [0,1]^2$ — which is exactly what a DTW path is, and is the natural completion of $\Gamma$ under jumps. Horizontal and vertical segments are precisely cuts on one side or the other.

Then the whole thing is one functional:

$$ E[R] \;=\; \underbrace{\int_R \big\lVert q_f - q_g \big\rVert^2}_{\text{fit}} \;+\; \underbrace{\lambda \int \big|\log \dot\gamma\big|^2}_{\text{rubato}} \;+\; \underbrace{\mu\,\#\{\text{jumps}\}}_{\text{parsimony}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{\text{jumps}} \int_{\text{excised}} \lVert f \rVert_V}_{\text{mass destroyed}} $$

Reading the terms: the third is your "minimal set of transformations" (an affine gap opening cost, so one long cut beats many short ones); the fourth is §2, and is what makes snipping at the edge of a rest free. $\log\dot\gamma$ rather than $\dot\gamma$ because tempo is multiplicative — twice as fast and half as fast should cost the same, which your current $\int|\varphi'|^2$ does not deliver.

6. The 89th coordinate — three caveats

performance.py already builds $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{88}$ as a step function in $BV \cap L^\infty$, with pedal kept as a separate track. So promoting it to a coordinate is a real proposal. Three things to get right:

issuewhat it means
Pedal is not a source It does not add sound; it modifies decay. $\mathbb{R}^{88}\oplus\mathbb{R}$ presents as a direct sum something that acts multiplicatively — closer to a bundle or a semidirect product. For alignment a weighted 89th coordinate is harmless. For synthesis it is wrong, and your synth/ already treats it correctly.
Make it continuous performance.py thresholds CC64 ≥ 64 and says half-pedalling is not modelled. But the FP-30X sends continuous CC64 — the 2026-08-24 take contains values of 18 and 3 among its 5,950 pedal messages. The binary version is discarding real technique. An 89th coordinate valued in $[0,1]$ recovers it.
Velocity as amplitude is lossy $P$ enters as the height of an indicator, but velocity changes timbre, not just level — your fitted acoustic preset carries velocity_brightness = 1.61. A scalar coordinate is a summary of a spectral change. Fine for alignment; do not mistake it for the sound.

7. The caveat that matters most

This machinery fixes warping. It does not fix discrimination — and discrimination is what failed.

Two separable problems have been sharing a name:

  • Which piece / where in it? A question about the feature map. It failed because $\pi$ has a 76-dimensional kernel — precision at $z\ge6$ fell from 0.979 against 24 decoys to 0.544 against 515. Richer transformations cannot recover information a projection already destroyed. Cut-and-glue will not help here at all; using more of $V$ than its octave quotient will.
  • Given the passage, how do the two readings correspond? A question about the transformation class, and it is genuinely limited by the homeomorphism assumption — restarts, repeats of a hard bar, and stopping mid-phrase are all jumps. Everything above applies, and this is where it pays.

Which is convenient, because the studio's verify step is the second question, not the first: you declare the bars, so the piece and the passage are known, and all that remains is the correspondence. Jump-aware alignment is the right model for practice, where homeomorphisms never were — Nakamura et al. built HMMs over score position with exactly these skip-and-repeat transitions for the same reason.
